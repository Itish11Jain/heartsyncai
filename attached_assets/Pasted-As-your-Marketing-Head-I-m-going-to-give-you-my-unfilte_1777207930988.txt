As your Marketing Head, I’m going to give you my unfiltered, critical UX (User Experience) assessment of these two screens.

First, the positive: Visually, your UI designer nailed it. The dark mode, the gradients, the typography—it looks like a premium, native iOS app. It builds instant brand trust.

Now, the critical problem: You have built a beautiful waiting room, but the door to the clinic is closed. Here is my ruthless breakdown of why these two screens are killing your 4,000 clicks, and the exact UI solution to fix it.

The Critical UX Tear-Down
Screen 1 (The Main Landing Page): The "Gatekeeper" Trap

The Flaw: When a user clicks your Instagram ad, they have already decided they want to interact with the card. By dropping them on this page, you are forcing them to wait for a 3D animation to load, read a headline, and then make a second decision to click that big pink/orange button.

The Psychology: Every click you ask a user to make cuts your conversion rate in half. You are making them click a button just to start the process they already clicked the ad to do.

The Verdict: This page is beautiful for an investor deck, but terrible for cold Instagram ad traffic.

Screen 2 (The /send Form): The "Survey" Vibe

The Flaw: If you send traffic directly to this second screen, you break the visual promise of the ad. The ad promised floating gold envelopes and magic. This screen looks like a multiple-choice survey or a settings menu.

The Psychology: Asking "What's the occasion?" forces cognitive load. The user has to stop and think, "Wait, it's not really her birthday, it's just a random Tuesday... should I click 'Feel Good' or is there a 'Just Because' option?" The moment they have to think hard, they bounce.

The Verdict: It's too dry, and it asks a high-friction question first.

The Solution: The "Merged Micro-Commitment" UI
We need to delete the gap between Screen 1 and Screen 2. We are going to merge the beautiful aesthetic of the first screen with a zero-friction action.

Here is exactly what your developer/designer needs to build for the new unified landing page:

1. The Fake 3D Hero (Speed Hack)
Keep the beautiful UI of Screen 1. However, remove the actual WebGL/Three.js code from this specific page. Replace that central animated card with a high-quality, auto-playing .mp4 or .webm looping video of the 3D animation.

Why? A compressed looping video looks exactly like the real 3D animation but loads in 0.2 seconds. It gives them the "magic" instantly without crashing their Android phone.

2. The "Zero-Click" Input (The Golden Fix)
Remove the big pink "Send a card in 20 seconds" button completely. Do not make them click to start.
Instead, replace that button with a large, glowing text input field directly on this homepage.

The layout should look like this:

Send love in a card.
(Looping lightweight video of the card magic)

Who do you want to surprise today?
[ Type their name here...          ]  <- This is an active text box
[ Let's Go ✨ ] <- Submit button

3. Change the First Question
Notice how I changed the first question from "What's the occasion?" to "Who is it for (Name)?"

The Psychology: Typing a name requires zero cognitive load. It is an automatic reflex. If I want to send a card to Priya, typing "Priya" is effortless.

The Hook: Once they type "Priya" and hit "Let's Go," then you load the real 3D engine in the background and slide them over to your Screen 2 to pick the occasion. Because they have already invested the effort of typing her name, their brain says, "I've already started, I might as well finish."

Summary of the Action Plan:
Combine the two screens. Give them the visual magic immediately (using video, not code), and put the simplest possible text input (Name) right in front of their thumbs before they have to click a single button.

If you make this UI change while your tech team halves the backend load times, your conversion rate will skyrocket when we turn those ads back on.
